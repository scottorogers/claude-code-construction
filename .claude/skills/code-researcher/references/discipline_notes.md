# Code Researcher — Discipline Notes (UK)

**On nation and version — never assume:**
Approved Documents apply to England. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland run
their own regimes. Within England, the version of an Approved Document that
applies depends on its transitional provisions and the date the building
control application or initial notice was made. A requirement confirmed against
the wrong version is not confirmed. Establish nation, version and
higher-risk-building status first (Phase 3a).

**On Approved Documents vs regulations:**
The legal requirement is the regulation (e.g. requirement B1 in Schedule 1).
The Approved Document is guidance on one way to meet it. A fire-engineered
solution or BS 9999 route can be valid even where it departs from AD B figures.
If the fire strategy uses another route, research against that route, not AD B.

**On standards:**
BS, BS EN and BS 7671 text is paywalled. Cite the standard number and the
edition the spec names. Mark clause-level content `[VERIFY]` unless the user
supplies the clause. A standard cited with no edition is itself a finding.

**On scope boundaries:**
The gap analysis is only as good as the documents reviewed in Pass 1. If the
engineer's scope includes drawings that weren't provided, or spec sections that
weren't read, those gaps cannot be identified. State clearly in the report
which documents were reviewed and which were not.

**On other authorities:**
Building control is not the only authority. Depending on scope, relevant bodies
include: the fire and rescue service (consulted through building control; also
enforcing the Fire Safety Order once occupied), environmental health (catering,
food hygiene), the water undertaker (Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations),
the DNO (connections, substations), the Health and Safety Executive (CDM, work
at height, electricity at work), the planning authority (conditions), the
Building Safety Regulator (higher-risk buildings) and the client's insurer.
Flag the possibility of an overlay whenever the scope involves catering,
healthcare, hazardous materials, fuel or gas, lifts, pressure systems, data
centres, or work on the public highway.

**On Pass 1 completeness:**
Before declaring a topic "not addressed," confirm that the relevant spec
section was read in full, that related sections were checked (Preliminaries and
general M&E sections often cover topics globally), that the fire strategy was
read, and that relevant drawing notes and schedules were reviewed. A premature
"not found" in Pass 1 becomes a false positive gap in Pass 3.

**On confidence levels:**
`confirmed` means: (1) the nation, document version and edition have been
verified, (2) the paragraph, table or regulation has been read from the source
(not paraphrased from memory or a secondary site), (3) the project document
status has been verified by reading the actual document. Anything short of all
three is `needs_review` or `uncertain`.
